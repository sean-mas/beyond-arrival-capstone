Two things surprise most families, and the second one surprises people from the United
States and the United Kingdom in particular.

The first is that school attendance is not optional in the way it may be where you came
from. The obligation attaches to living in the state, not to your nationality or
immigration status, so it applies to your child immediately. And it is an obligation to
attend an actual school. Home schooling does not satisfy it. Germany is unusual in this
and it is not a grey area.

The second is how a child with little German is usually placed. Most states run
preparatory classes, under different names depending on where you are, where newly
arrived children learn German intensively while joining regular lessons for subjects
like sport, art and music from the start. In Berlin these are Willkommensklassen,
normally lasting up to a year, generally from about age eight, with younger children
going straight into the first years of primary school.

## What to do

Contact the school office for your district rather than individual schools. In most
places that office assesses the child's language, arranges a school medical check and
assigns the place, so approaching schools directly sends you back a step.

Ask specifically what language support the assigned school provides and for how long,
because this varies more between schools than between states.

Ask how and when the child moves into a regular class, and on what basis that is decided.

## If a child stays behind to finish school

The obligation works the other way round too. A child who stays in your home country to
finish school there, with a parent or with relatives, does not live in the state, so the
German obligation does not reach them until they move here. If you are planning this,
say so to the district school office when the rest of the family registers, and ask how
they treat a child who is registered at your address but still living abroad.

## What varies

Everything above varies by state and often by district: what the class is called, the
age thresholds, how long it lasts, which office you contact. Treat Berlin as an example
rather than the rule and ask what applies where you live.

If your child has been without a school place for weeks, raise that with a person.
