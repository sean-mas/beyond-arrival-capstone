The first question is not how to get recognised. It is whether you need to be.

German professions split in two. Regulated professions, mostly in health, law, teaching
and safety, cannot be practised without recognition at all. Doctor, nurse, teacher,
lawyer. For everything else, and that is most degrees and most trades, you may work
without recognition. Nobody is legally protecting the title.

If you are in the second group you may be able to start applying for work today while
any paperwork runs in the background. A good number of people spend months on
recognition that was never a gate for them.

## What to do

Start at anerkennung-in-deutschland.de. It will tell you whether your profession is
regulated and which authority is responsible for it.

If it is not regulated, apply for work now. Recognition can still help, because
employers read a German certificate more easily and it can affect pay, but it is
optional rather than a condition.

If it is regulated, expect the language requirement to be part of the assessment rather
than separate from it, and start collecting certified translations early because that
step is slow and nobody warns you about it.

## One structural thing to expect

Some professions, including teacher and engineer, are regulated at state level rather
than federal level. That means sixteen different sets of rules, sixteen authorities, and
a recognition granted in one state that does not automatically travel with you to
another. If you might move within Germany, ask about that before you start.

The portal indicates three to four months from complete documents and fees that can run
to several hundred euros. Treat both as indicative.
