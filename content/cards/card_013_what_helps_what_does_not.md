Most advice about settling in is guesswork. Some of it has been measured, and two
findings are worth having.

The first is about coping. A study of 191 couples across 37 countries compared partners
who coped by acting on the problem in front of them against partners who coped by
managing how they felt about it. The first group adjusted better on every measure. The
second did worse. That is not an instruction to suppress anything. It means that on a
bad week, doing one concrete thing does more than getting your head right about it
first.

The second is about what settling in even means. It separates into your work situation,
your interactions with people here, and general daily living, and these move
independently. Being completely comfortable in the supermarket while having nobody to
talk to is not a contradiction. It is the normal shape.

## What people actually do

Keep one small thing moving on a bad day. Send the email, attend the class, make the
appointment. The size does not matter and the outcome does not matter. The acting does.

Track the three areas separately rather than asking how it is going overall. People who
feel they are failing at everything are usually failing at one of the three and
succeeding at another, and cannot see it because they have merged them.

Expect the interaction side to be slowest. It is the one that depends on other people's
calendars rather than on your effort.

## What to ignore

The idea of a honeymoon, then a crisis at a predictable month, then recovery. When it
was tested over a year, difficulty was highest at the beginning and declined. There is
no month you are behind schedule for, and anyone giving you a timeline is inventing it.
