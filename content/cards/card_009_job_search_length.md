If you assumed you would find something within three months and you are now at six, you
have not failed at something everyone else manages. In one community poll of 132 people,
half said their search had taken more than six months.

Three things make it slower here, and none of them are about you. German is the first:
plenty of adverts say English is fine and plenty of those teams still run their meetings
in German, so the level required is often not the level advertised. The second is that
your network is gone, and most roles move through people who already know someone. The
third is that German hiring reads a CV differently, weighting certificates, formal
qualifications and continuous employment more heavily, and reading gaps more literally.

## What people actually do

Check first whether your profession is regulated. If it is not, and most are not, you
can apply today and let any recognition paperwork run in the background. People lose
months to a process that was never a gate for them.

Put the German course in the calendar as a work task rather than a personal project.
It is the single input that moves both the job search and the social side, and it is the
one thing entirely within your control.

Treat the Bundesagentur für Arbeit as a real channel rather than a formality. It is free,
it holds vacancies employers do not post elsewhere, and registration is separate from
claiming anything.

Apply in German even imperfectly where the advert is in German. A slightly awkward
German application signals intent in a way a polished English one does not.

## What does not work

Waiting until your German is good enough before starting, and applying only through
international job boards, which is where the most competition and the fewest roles are.
