There is a state funded integration course, and two separate things people confuse about
it.

The first is entitlement. If you received a first residence permit for work or family
reasons you generally have a one off entitlement to a place, and it expires one year
after your permit is issued unless you were prevented from registering for reasons
outside your control. People discover this in month fourteen.

The second is obligation. Being entitled and being required are different. If you are
entitled but cannot yet communicate in German in at least a simple way, attendance can
be mandatory, and culpable non-attendance or failing the final test can be taken into
account when your residence permit comes up for extension.

Here is the counter-intuitive part that catches spouses. If your partner holds an EU Blue
Card or a skilled worker permit you were exempt from proving German before you came.
That exemption is about entry. It does not exempt you from the obligation once you are
here.

## What to do

Register within the first year of your permit, whether or not you feel ready, because
the entitlement expires and the place does not have to be used immediately.

Ask about the Kostenbeitrag exemption if money is the obstacle. Exemptions exist for
hardship and for benefit recipients, and half the contribution is refunded if you pass
within two years.

Ask your employer whether they pay it. Many do, but that is a matter of your contract
rather than of law, so it has to be asked for.

## What the course is

600 hours of language teaching plus 100 hours on how the country works, aiming at B1.
The contribution is 2,29 euros per teaching hour at the time of writing, so a 700 hour
course is roughly 1.600 euros.
