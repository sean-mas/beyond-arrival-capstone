Your foreign licence is valid here for six months. The clock starts when you take up
ordinary residence, not when you land and not when you register, though in practice
those are usually close.

The reason to take this seriously is what happens afterwards. The licence does not
quietly become invalid in a way that earns you a ticket. Driving on it on day two
hundred is driving without a licence, which is a criminal matter rather than a traffic
offence, and your insurance is affected too.

There is an extension of up to six more months, but it is designed for people who can
show they are leaving Germany within twelve months. It is not an extension for people
who are settling.

## What to do, and when

In month four, look up your own licence in Germany's country list. This is more granular
than people expect. For the United States, Canada and Australia the answer is decided
per state or province rather than per country, and several US states require the theory
test.

Then book at the Fahrerlaubnisbehörde. Whether you need no test, the theory test, the
practical test or both follows from that list rather than from anything you can
negotiate.

If a test is required, start driving school immediately. Driving schools have waiting
times and those waiting times are inside your six months, not added to them.

## What does not work

Starting in month six. By then the queue alone can take you past the deadline, and there
is no provision for having tried. If you depend on driving for work or a school run,
treat month four as the hard start.
