The boxes are unpacked. The admin is mostly done. Your partner leaves in the morning
with somewhere to be, and the day in front of you has nothing in it that has to happen.

People describe this in almost identical words. One described hours to fill with
nothing that had to happen in any particular order. None of them were in
crisis. They were a few months in and had lost the thing nobody counts as a thing: a
reason the day has to start. What you lost is not only a job, if you had one. It is
structure, and being someone with a role rather than someone accompanying a role.
Research on accompanying partners names this specifically as a threat to identity,
separate from the practical side of moving.

## What people actually do

Look for your local Volkshochschule. Every town and district in Germany has one. They
are municipal adult education centres, they are cheap, and everything runs on a fixed
weekly timetable, which is the part that matters. German courses there fill up months
in advance, but hobby and craft courses usually do not.

A Verein rather than a gym. A gym is anonymous by design and you can attend for a year
without anyone learning your name. A Verein has membership, a fixed training evening,
and the same faces every week, which is how contact accumulates here.

If you have children in a Kita or a school, the other parents are the one group you are
already inside without having to arrange anything.

Pick one thing at a fixed time. Two is a plan, and plans fail in a week where you feel
like this.

## What does not work

Waiting until you feel better before starting. Waiting until your German is good enough,
because a course is where the German comes from. And trying to make it happen at the
playground or the supermarket, where you never see the same person twice.
