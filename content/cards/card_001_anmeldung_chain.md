The law gives you two weeks from the day you move in to register your address. Not from
your arrival in Germany, not from the start of your lease. From the day you actually
move in.

You cannot do this alone. Whoever provides the flat has to give you a signed
confirmation that you live there, and if you are subletting that is your main tenant.
They are legally obliged to provide it and they have the same two weeks.

The reason to treat this as urgent is not the fine. There is a legal maximum of 1.000
euros for registering late, but whether anything is charged is up to the municipality
and often nothing is. The real reason is that almost everything else waits behind this
one appointment.

## What to do, in order

Book the appointment before you move if you can, because in several large cities the
calendar is fuller than the two week deadline allows.

Get the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung in writing from your landlord at the same time as the
keys. It has to name the landlord, the address, the move in date and everyone
registering.

Register, then wait for your tax identification number, which is generated
automatically once the registry passes your details on and arrives by post.

Then the rest: bank, health insurer, any Kita application. All of them ask for the
registration confirmation.

## Why the order matters

Until your employer has your tax number, your salary is taxed at the highest bracket and
you get the difference back only later. That is a bigger practical cost than the fine,
and it is the actual reason to register promptly.

If you are already past two weeks you are not the first. Register when you can get in,
and keep evidence of when you first tried to book.
