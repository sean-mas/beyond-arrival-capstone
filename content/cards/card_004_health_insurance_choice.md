Everyone living in Germany must have health insurance. That part is simple. The choice
underneath it is not, and it is close to permanent, so it is worth slowing down for.

If you are employed you are normally in the statutory system automatically. You can only
leave it if your income is above a threshold set each year, which for 2026 is 77.400
euros.

## The difference that matters for a family

In the statutory system, a spouse who is not working and any children can usually be
covered at no extra premium. In the private system every family member needs their own
policy, and the premium is based on age and health rather than income.

So a single earner family with children often finds the arithmetic goes one way in the
first year and the other way later, as premiums rise with age and the family grows.

## Why this is not a normal decision

Going back is the hard part. The health ministry states that an arbitrary switch from
private back to statutory is excluded. Return is possible only if your circumstances
change so that compulsory statutory insurance applies again, and from age 55 the law
largely closes that door altogether.

Brokers are paid commission on private policies. That does not make their advice wrong,
but it is worth knowing before the conversation rather than after.

## What to do

Ask us what the mechanism is, and ask an independent adviser what you should do. That
distinction is not modesty. This is a decision with decades of consequences and a
product cannot responsibly make it for you.

If anyone is pressing you to decide this week, that pressure is not coming from the law.
