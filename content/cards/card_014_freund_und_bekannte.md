One piece of vocabulary explains most of the frustration, and almost nobody gets told
it.

In English, friend stretches a long way. It covers someone you have known twenty years
and someone you met twice and like. German splits that. A Freund is a small category
built slowly and held for a long time. Everyone else is a Bekannte, an acquaintance,
which is a normal and stable place to be rather than a waiting room. As one person with
two decades here put it, everybody else is a Bekannte, which translates as acquaintance.

So the thing that feels like rejection, someone being warm with you for months without
it becoming anything, is usually not rejection. You are being treated correctly for the
category you are in. The category just moves more slowly than you are used to. In one
large survey, 43 percent of internationals said locals were generally friendly, which is
low, so the difficulty is not imagined.

## What people actually do

Join something with a register and a fixed night. A Verein, a sports club, a choir, a
Volkshochschule course, a regular volunteering slot. Germany has very little casual
acquaintance layer, so you enter through a formal membership rather than by bumping into
people.

Turn up to the same thing repeatedly for months before expecting anything. The mechanism
is repetition, not charm, and people who found their way in describe it the same way
every time.

Accept invitations exactly as issued, and issue them precisely. A vague "we should do
something" reads as politeness here rather than as an opening. A specific day and time
reads as real.

## What does not work

Expecting warmth to convert into friendship on its own, and reading the slowness as a
verdict on you.
