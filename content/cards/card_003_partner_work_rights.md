This is the most confused question on every forum, so here is the general position.

If you came to Germany to join your spouse and you hold a residence permit for that
reason, you are allowed to work. The law changed in 2020 so that holding a residence
title permits employment unless some law specifically forbids it, and no law forbids it
here. The Federal Office for Migration states it plainly: as the person who joined, you
are entitled to work.

The permission is not restricted. Not to one employer, not to one occupation, not to a
maximum number of hours, and it covers self-employment as well as being employed. You do
not apply separately for a work permit. It comes with the residence title itself.

## What to check

Look at your residence card. It should carry wording permitting employment. If it does
not, that is worth raising rather than assuming you are not allowed.

Know which permit your spouse holds. The exemption from proving basic German before
entering Germany attaches to their permit type, an EU Blue Card or a skilled worker
permit among others, rather than to your nationality. If they later change permit type,
the position can change with it.

## What this does not solve

Being permitted to work and finding work are different problems and the second one is
real. It is taking most people longer than they expected, for reasons that are mostly
structural. That is a separate conversation and we have one.

Being exempt from proving German before entry is also not the same as being exempt from
the obligation to attend an integration course once you are here. Those are two
different rules and people routinely conflate them.
