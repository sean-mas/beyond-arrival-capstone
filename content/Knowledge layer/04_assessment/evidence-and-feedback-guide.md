# Evidence and Feedback Guide

Assessment in 2026 moves beyond simplistic "cultural knowledge quizzes." Intercultural Competence (IC) must be evaluated as a multidimensional, longitudinal process.

## Types of Evidence

1. **Reflective Portfolios (Cognitive & Affective)**
   - *What it is:* Written, audio, or video logs where learners analyze their intercultural encounters over time.
   - *What to look for:* Evidence of the OSEE cycle (separating observation from interpretation), increasing self-awareness, and shifts in attitude (e.g., from frustration to curiosity).

2. **Observed Interaction Tasks (Behavioral & Communicative)**
   - *What it is:* Role-plays, tele-simulations, or real-world project observations.
   - *What to look for:* Real-time adaptation. Did they change their communication style when confusion arose? Did they build rapport successfully?

3. **360-Degree / Peer Feedback**
   - *What it is:* Feedback from intercultural partners, team members, and facilitators.
   - *What to look for:* How is the learner perceived by *others* across cultural lines? (Impact > Intent).

## How to Avoid Narrow Metrics
- **Do not use static scores:** A single numerical score (e.g., 85/100 on cultural intelligence) is insufficient and often misleading.
- **Focus on the delta:** Measure the *change* in complexity of thought and behavior over a 3-6 month period.
- **Incorporate reflexivity:** The highest level of assessment requires the learner to evaluate their own biases and structural power.
