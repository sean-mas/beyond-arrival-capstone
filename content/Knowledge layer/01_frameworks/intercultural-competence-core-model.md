# Intercultural Competence Core Model

Intercultural competence (IC) is the ability to interact appropriately and effectively across cultural identities toward shared goals, emphasizing knowledge, attitudes, skills, and communicative performance.

## The 4 Domains of Competence

1. **Cognitive (Knowledge/Understanding)**
   - *Definition:* Understanding of self, others, and systemic cultural processes.
   - *Key Skills:* Cultural self-awareness, deep cultural knowledge (beyond surface traits), understanding of socio-linguistic contexts.
   
2. **Affective (Attitudes)**
   - *Definition:* The emotional readiness and disposition toward intercultural engagement.
   - *Key Skills:* Openness, curiosity, empathy, tolerance for ambiguity, withholding judgment.

3. **Behavioral (Skills)**
   - *Definition:* The adaptation strategies and actions taken during interactions.
   - *Key Skills:* Perspective-taking, behavioral flexibility, relationship-building, conflict resolution.

4. **Communicative (Pragmatic/Linguistic Performance - ICC)**
   - *Definition:* The ability to achieve communicative goals across differences while maintaining respect and dignity.
   - *Key Skills:* Active listening, adapting communication style, navigating language barriers, decoding non-verbal cues.

## Canonical vs. Dynamic Paradigms
While classic 2012-era experiential models focused heavily on "understanding the other", the 2026 paradigm shifts toward **dynamic, multi-level processes**. IC is not a static endpoint but a lifelong process of critical reflection on one's own positionality, systemic power structures, and reciprocal relationships.
