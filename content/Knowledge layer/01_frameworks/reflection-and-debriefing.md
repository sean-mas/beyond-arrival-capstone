# Learning Design and Facilitation Principles

Intercultural competence development is strongest when there is **structured intercultural contact, guided reflection, and facilitation/feedback**, rather than content delivery alone.

## Design Heuristics
1. **Always pair experience with reflection.** (Experience alone does not equal learning).
2. **Prioritize contact + guided debrief.**
3. **Pace the emotional and cognitive load.** (Start low-risk, move to high-risk).

## The Facilitation Cycle (Setup and Debrief)

### 1. Setup
- **Frame the Concept:** Connect the activity to the broader context (e.g., "Today we are looking at ambiguity").
- **Provide Cultural Data/Context:** Set the scene without relying on stereotypes.
- **Inoculate Against Resistance:** Acknowledge that this might feel uncomfortable or that there are no perfect answers.
- **State Objectives:** Clear learning goals mapped to the 4 domains.

### 2. Debrief (The OSEE / DAE Approach)
Do not skip the debrief. Use structured questioning:
- **Observe:** What exactly happened? What were the facts? (Remove interpretation).
- **Interpret / Analyze:** What are the multiple possible meanings of what happened? 
- **Evaluate / Apply:** How did this make you feel? What is the most culturally appropriate response? How will you use this in the future?

## Facilitation Scripts for Agents
*AI Agents acting as facilitators should use these prompts:*
- "Before we judge the scenario, let's list just the observable facts. What did you see or read?"
- "Can you generate two alternative explanations for this behavior that don't rely on negative intent?"
