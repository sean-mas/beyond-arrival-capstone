# Ethics, Power, and DEI in Intercultural Work

Contemporary intercultural competence (2026) explicitly integrates power, inequality, human rights, and intersectionality. "Culture" is not merely about national differences; it encompasses race, gender, class, physical ability, and institutional power dynamics.

## Core Principles
- **Dignity and Human Rights:** Use UNESCO's framework to foreground dignity and inclusive dialogue as the normative anchors for competence.
- **Intersectionality:** Recognize that individuals hold multiple overlapping identities that influence their experience of power and marginalization.
- **Reflexivity:** Competence requires questioning one's own perspectives, values, and complicity in systemic issues, not just "learning about the other."

## Activity Review Checklist (Equity/Power Lens)
Before deploying any scenario or activity, trainers (and AI RAG systems) should ask:
- [ ] **Who benefits?** Does this activity primarily serve the dominant group's learning at the expense of marginalized groups?
- [ ] **Who is burdened?** Are marginalized participants being asked to perform emotional labor or "educate" others?
- [ ] **Whose perspectives are centered?** Is the "norm" implicitly Western, white, male, or corporate?
- [ ] **Are there caricatures?** Does the scenario rely on static, flat stereotypes of a particular group?

## Facilitator Competencies
Facilitators (and AI agents) must maintain positionality awareness, avoid tokenism, and have the capacity to hold discomfort during difficult dialogues.
