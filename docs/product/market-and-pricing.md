That last line is the market definition that matters. Not a category size: eleven companies
out of a counted pool of sixteen hundred.

**Track B · Families direct (the B2C market)**

| | |
|---|---|
| Pool | 15 expats.de Facebook groups, roughly 500k memberships with heavy overlap; r/expats ~251k (niche:ev_19) |
| Price tested | EUR 19 per month |
| Subscribers needed for 5k | ~263 |

Track B carries a fatal structural objection (rt8): the product succeeds by making itself
unnecessary, so lifetime value caps near EUR 228. It is being run as a **demand test**, not
as a chosen business model. If nobody pays EUR 19, the churn arithmetic never matters.

## The arithmetic, from the T2 decision memo

Eleven 50-seat accounts clears EUR 5,000 a month, against a counted pool of 1,659 German
relocation providers. Sized on software anchors only, per constraint C1 in
`04-side-project/constraints.md`.

**What the memo says the evidence does not support**, and it has not changed:

- **The differentiator is not AI.** Country Navigator ships an AI cultural coach as a
  standard inclusion at the same EUR 115 per user per year.
- **The territory is occupied.** Benivo sells a spouse Partner Platform, SIRVA bundles
  cultural training, ICUnet sells the whole thing as a service in Germany. Competition
  scored 1 of 3 and that is the real ceiling on this idea.
- **Demand evidence is one step removed.** ECA measures mobility teams adopting AI, not
  families wanting a companion.

**Price for this exact job remains unobserved.** Willingness to pay scores 2 of 3 for that
reason, and only a provider call settles it.
